The Moral Realization and My Theory of Intelligence

It all started when I was in my 2005 Silver Mustang, with my girlfriend Lia in the passenger seat, driving home from that Laker game.

I was thinking about how surreal this date was, how it couldn't have gone any better, and how I wouldn't rather be with anyone else.

I may have been naive, but I felt self-actualized, like I had achieved both riches and love. You know the expression when something is "too quiet"? Well this date felt "too perfect". Something didn't feel right. As I looked at the faces of the drivers of the other cars, I began to experience sonder.

My life at that moment couldn't have been more perfect if I had written it down ahead of time. "I want Kobe to hit a buzzer beater over Dwayne Wade, and I want to go home with the woman of my dreams". So I began to consider, if my dreams are becoming real, should I include these other drivers into my dream reality? Do I have a responsibility to share my power of manifestation that is genuinely puzzling me?

I used my theory of mind to imagine the stories behind the countenances on the drivers around me. I noticed the architecture, if you can call it that, visible on the sides of the freeway, and I pondered the stark contrast in quality between the beauty of my date, and the ugliness of the city. The equivalent match for her would be beautiful pyramids and cathedrals.

We had a policy invented by my yearbook winning girlfriend, where I could only call her beautiful when I first saw her (so once per date) otherwise we were in danger of me speaking of nothing else. During the carride home, I asked her if she could momentarily suspend this rule, so I could fully explain to her how beautiful I knew she was.

I asked Lia if she loved me. I remember giving her permission to change her answer once she’d heard me out. The car, the tickets, my personality on a date: I told her how much of that ingenuity I’d spent on getting to sleep with her and keeping that privilege for life. I wanted to marry her. I also knew the incumbent boyfriend could get lazy and count on being forgiven. I wanted credit for admitting that before I’d even done it.

She stayed and asked questions. One answer still bothers me to write. I said that if she lost the ability to speak, I thought I could be almost as happy, provided she understood me and did as I asked. Her jaw dropped. I hurried to tell her how much I liked talking to her. Of all the things to say to the woman whose voice I’d spend years trying to remember.

My theory was sexual right down to its definition of intelligence. What did a man want most? Whom did she want? I took Lia choosing me as the strongest evidence I had about my own mind. There’s a spectacular leap between “the most beautiful woman I know loves me” and “I might be the smartest man alive.” At eighteen I was prepared to submit that leap for testing. Introduce me to a better man with a better theory and I could go home, marry her, raise a family. Otherwise I wanted to donate my brain. In It’s a Wonderful Life George offers Mary the moon on a lasso. I told Lia I didn’t know about the moon. I thought I could get her this planet if she followed my lead.

In the story I remember, nobody ever brought that better man through the door. No name, no introduction. What came back to me was the answer that they would go ahead with my plan. I had hoped for the meeting more than I let on.

There was a recording on her iPhone

The first recording was on her iPhone. I said I was eighteen, of sound mind, willing to risk my life. I’d thought about how an intelligence agency might dismiss an unsolicited recording from a kid. Lia knew the officer I wanted to hear it, my friend’s father. I had a hunch he knew somebody who would listen. The phone was hers.

The part I find hardest to put on the page is what I asked of her. I wanted her to offer the officer oral sex and ask him to hear the recording through. In my theory even securing an audience had to demonstrate the power of desire. I called that a mission. I can tell you what I remember asking. I wasn’t there when she took the phone away, and I have no account of that encounter from either person.

I thought the game fell on a weekday. That left a few ordinary days before our next date. I wanted psychiatrists to look at my life in that interval and commit their judgments to paper before they heard the memo. I wanted to know what a recording could change about their reading of the same week. Even then I liked arranging the order in which other people got their evidence.

When she had to decide whether to carry it, there was one memo. In it I offered three things for inspection:

  1. A man of legal age and sound mind consenting to experimentation up to and including death for the benefit of humanity
  2. Legitimate credentials on intelligence such as my experience in GATE "gifted and talented education" and my SAT score
  3. My claim that the most beautiful girl in Westlake Village would choose to carry my words

My proposed search ran backwards from the usual intelligence test. Find the women everybody wants, ask whom they want, and study those men. I thought my failure to attract a recruiter meant the agencies had missed something, or that someone was waiting to see what I’d do. Even within the story I couldn’t tell whether I was inventing the Alpha Program or applying to it.

Keep in mind, the recording is going for shock and awe, so I used riddles. For example I remember saying in the intial recording, "They'll want to know my SAT score. Tell them I got 2400 but swapped two of the digits around to get better than a perfect score. Have them take a multiple choice test with the possible permutations. Make I don't know the last option. If they guess incorrectly my score, send them away and tell them I said they are idiots. If they guess right, Keep them, and if they say they don't know, keep them too, for they are the smartest".

My answer, which I saved for the second recording, was 2040. The missing 360 points belonged to Audrey, the cheerleader in the testing room. I got her number. We dated. I broke her heart. In the sexual joke I told about our time together, one full turn gave me my other 360. That was my scoring system: if a perfect score cost me the date, I wanted the 2040. Then I heard myself say, “If that doesn't make me God, I don't know what does”. Some distance to travel on a math joke.

Lia and I had already talked about how being together felt like Heaven. “If this is Heaven, then I would be God” was the leap I made out loud. One small object kept coming up whenever I tried to explain why that leap felt possible.

There was a die.

A classmate had a big fluffy die. I offered five dollars. He wanted the die. So did I. I offered to bet the five that it would come up five. He still said no. I got the bill out. “It's yours, all you have to do is not roll a 5”. I’ve forgotten which of us rolled. I remember the five. I may even have let him keep the money. What stuck with me was how many times I had already put that number into both our heads before the die left anybody’s hand.

I filed it with a winning streak at Sorry, a whole day at Magic Mountain settling things by rock-paper-scissors, a three-round card game that ended on exactly 5,000, and a win at poker. In my theory the stakes had to matter. A roll for fun could never settle the question. A skeptic can probably hear the difficulty with my experiment in that last sentence.

The boy coasting through class might have been working very hard on the thing he actually wanted. I thought that was the intelligence a test missed. I carried the thought into a theory about attractiveness, intelligence and whom people have children with. I had my own desires, one relationship, and an astonishing amount of confidence about everybody else.

There will be a family

“The principle threat is bullets to the head” is how I remember saying it. I was offering them my brain, and my mind went to a family hearing that somebody they loved had died under friendly fire.

“There will be a family.” I wanted someone to hear a family’s suspicion of wrongful death and take it seriously. In the recording I invoked Pat Tillman. I imagined an order behind his death, demanded immediate action, and even spoke of torture to get a confession. That was the frightening part of my reasoning. I was asking strangers to accept my certainty in place of an investigation.

“You can't beat Pat Tillman in a fair fight. Therefore they cheated.” I used to call that a logically valid statement. On paper I can see the missing step. It tells you the kind of hero Tillman was to me and what I could imagine him refusing to do to his brothers. It tells you nothing about who gave an order. In the mythology the second recording folds back on itself here. People from different times get a turn to ask their questions in the same Mustang.

There is a chair

“There is a chair.” Rick and I were at his dad’s apartment. Two lousy seats, one good one. His dad was out. I suggested we take a picture, sit in his chair, put it back exactly how we found it. Rick stayed where he was. So did I. That’s my whole answer to why I wanted his dad as project manager. I’d seen the man hold a room he wasn’t in. I called it the transitive property of respect. I wanted a chair kept for him in every room where people spoke my name. Rick, who treated his sister well, was the person I wanted to consult about women.

I wanted the donation to be final and the terms to be mine. I was afraid that if I gave myself up the ordinary way, somebody who liked the existing arrangement would quietly get rid of me. The strangest qualification I offered the good guys was how well I understood my own capacity to do evil.

Just as my relationship with Lia was built on trust, and we never looked at each others phones, I wanted my organization to grow organically through trust.

I even imagined bringing a hired killer in, letting him see the strength of the organization, and releasing him. In the movie in my head the good guys had to choose the decent thing when the expedient thing would work just as well.

I said I was "whatever God needs me to be, including God" because I assumed the responsibilies of God as well as deferring to the omnipotent power in the sky as my God.

Bulltrue / Spitfire
