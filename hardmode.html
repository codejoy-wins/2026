Season 0, Episode 0: "The Ampule"

Starring: Taron Egerton as Cael

COLD OPEN

INT. MIAMI PENTHOUSE - NIGHT
A neon-lit skyline glows through floor-to-ceiling windows. A luxurious party pulses with energy. CAEL (30s, charismatic, confident, effortlessly magnetic) glides through the space, drawing admiration without effort. Women flirt openly, men offer handshakes that linger just a moment too long.

CAEL (V.O.)
Some people fight their way to the top. Me? I woke up here.

(Extended V.O.)
Sure, I had to fake a few signatures and charm a few snakes to get from where I started to where I am, but now that I'm here, the only times I sweat—

(Cut to Cael passionately with his stunning girlfriend)
(Cut to Cael relaxing alone in his private sauna)

—is when I choose to.

MONTAGE: Cael boxing aggressively at a private gym. Celebrating as his startup’s stock price skyrockets. Toasting champagne, whispering confidently into a woman's ear. His phone vibrates repeatedly, ignored each time.

The camera tightens on Cael’s face, catching an almost imperceptible flicker of unease.

TITLE SEQUENCE

Minimalistic, cryptic visuals interwoven with abstract shapes and hidden symbols. The central phrase emerges:

"Do not bend reality to serve you. Serve reality until it bends to serve all."

ACT 1

INT. LUXURY CAR - NIGHT
Cael checks his phone, sees a missed call from an UNKNOWN NUMBER. A text message reads simply: "You have not yet donated your gift." Cael scoffs, deleting it without hesitation.

INT. UPSCALE RESTAURANT - NIGHT
Cael smoothly negotiates a lucrative deal. The other executives shake hands respectfully. His phone vibrates again. Same UNKNOWN NUMBER. Irritated, he answers sarcastically.

CAEL
Let me guess—you're selling enlightenment?

VOICE (O.S.)
And what would your version of enlightenment be? More for you?

Cael pauses, unsettled. The line disconnects abruptly, leaving him speechless.

ACT 2

INT. TECH OFFICE - MORNING
Cael strides into his office confidently, noticing strange stares. His assistant appears anxious.

ASSISTANT
The funding—it's gone. The board reversed their decision last night.

Cael's confidence flickers, briefly shadowed by confusion.

INT. HIGH-END BAR - NIGHT
Cael, slightly shaken but covering it, approaches a stunning MODEL. She politely entertains his advances, then pauses thoughtfully.

MODEL
Do you ever feel... like you're just too lucky?

CAEL
Luck is just the universe recognizing greatness. (offers her his business card, extending off his fingers)

MODEL
Or maybe... instead of bending reality to your will, you could bend yourself, so reality can be strong enough to bend for someone else?

She walks away confidently. Cael is left stunned, still holding the card out like an idiot.

INT. CAEL'S PENTHOUSE - NIGHT
Alone, Cael stares into a cracked mirror. His phone buzzes again. He picks up hesitantly.

VOICE (O.S.)
The game was rigged in your favor. Until you stopped deserving it.

CAEL
Maybe I never really played the game. Maybe it was just mine all along.

VOICE (O.S.)
What would you do with your life if you knew you'd be successful?

CAEL
What I'm doing, you bastard. I'm already successful. You know how I know that? Because I don't have to call random numbers and harass them to make a living.

Hangs up. Says to himself, "Prick."

Cael checks his bank balance, all digits are only zeros and ones. The money is still there, but someone is fucking with him.

ACT 3

EXT. RAINY ROOFTOP - NIGHT
Drenched and desperate, Cael steps onto the roof, isolated and vulnerable. He stares down at his phone, finally speaking aloud to the emptiness.

CAEL
Okay. Whatever this is... I'm ready.

His phone buzzes.

MODEL
Drink?

CAEL
You never took my card How did you get this number?

MODEL
I have an identical memory. Table 7 at The Owl Night Club.

INT. DIM ROOM - NIGHT

MODEL
You’ll be dealing with me from here on out. Novelty sticks. Memory fights to keep the unusual. You'll need to forget this conversation ever happened. I can help you with that. I'll remain in your life as a contact in the entertainment industry. We'll be working on a project together.

CAEL what project?

MODEL Doesn't matter. It anti-matters.

Cael is confused. She places a dossier before him.

MODEL (CONT’D) Your aptitude is astronomical. Innate capabilities others spend decades acquiring—you squander. Your life could uplift millions. But you’ve used it to benefit one.

Cael scoffs, ego bruised.

CAEL And I suppose this founder you follow—he’s a saint?

MODEL (smiles) He would say he's an evil saint. But he's actually closer to a Guilty God.

CAEL What's he doing that's so noble?

MODEL You are only authorized to know one time what he's doing at this moment, noble or not. Do you want to use your one time now?

CAEL Yes.

MODEL I can tell you that right now he's in his bed crying over somebody who he thinks doesn't love him anymore, even though she wants nothing more than to go be with him.

CAEL He sounds pathetic.

MODEL You'll earn the right to judge him—after you've been judged. And just so we're clear, the only reason I'm here with you right now, instead of trying to comfort him myself, is because he wants it that way.

She slides an ampule across the table.

MODEL When you drink this, You'll pass out and wake up at your flat. Your existing girlfriend's beauty makes her a magnet for men like you. She has limited access to our operations. She has agreed to administer this chemical into your drinks whenever we want. We need to study your natural way of eliminating difficulties from your life, by elevating the difficulty artificially until your strategy becomes more pronounced. We have been monitoring you as close as we would a nuclear missile.

CAEL Wait, what? Why?

MODEL Because so far, you and other males like you, are the most dangerous force in the universe. Have you ever felt like things just happen for you the way you want them to? The Alpha Program is designed to prevent you from desiring evil, because you have demonstrated the ability to manifest your desired reality.

MODEL (CONT’D) When you wake up, you’ll think we just... partied. You’ll feel a hangover. A hole in your mind. Your girlfriend will scold you for partying with another girl, and will tell you to try to keep it professional between us. You'll be in a suggestible state for a bit, and by the time you're back on your feet, you'll think you signed a contract as a financial consultant for my entertainment contract, instead of a top secret alpha program. I'll be your handler. It isn't perfect -— echoes might linger. You may dream of truths you can’t place.

CAEL (grimly determined) Then let them echo. I'm going to prove to you that this founder isn't who you think he is.

Cael downs the ampule competitively.
MODEL (rolls her eyes) He set it up so that we can recieve alphas who want to donate, but he is the only one who ever has donated himself. The rest have to be coherced. He gave you more than a decade to do the right thing.

Cael downs the ampule, MODEL rolls her eyes.

Cael's eyes close slowly, heavily.

ACT 4

MONTAGE:
Cael’s financial assets freeze. A Fed starts bothering him. The Fed uses a different name every time.

Friends and business partners ghost him.

Every attempt to regain control only increases his isolation and discomfort.

INT. CAEL'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

A mysterious folder slips beneath Cael’s door. Inside are strange, coded texts, and notes referencing "cyberscripture"

CAEL "King without a crown." Charming.

Later: Alone, Cael reads another note seriously. Inspired, he anonymously assists a struggling neighbor. The act feels strangely powerful, deeply authentic.

FINAL IMAGE

TEXT: "You are not lost. You are founder."

Season 0, Episode 1: “The Man on the Stairs”

INT. CAEL’S APARTMENT — 2:13 A.M.

There were three things in Cael’s refrigerator.

Mustard. Champagne. An envelope.

He remembered buying two of them.

He stood barefoot in the cold light, considering whether someone had broken into his apartment or whether he had finally become the sort of man who refrigerated his mail.

The envelope read:

YOUR NEIGHBOR RETURNED THIS.

Inside was four hundred dollars.

He had slipped the money under Mrs. Vale’s door six hours earlier, tucked into a folded takeaway menu. No signature. No lecture about financial independence.

Just enough to keep her electricity on.

Beneath the cash was a note in cramped handwriting.

Paid it yesterday. You looked tired when I saw you. Buy some actual food.

Cael stared at it.

Then he looked up at the ceiling.

CAEL
Was that part of the fucking test?

The refrigerator hummed.

CAEL
Compelling rebuttal.

Someone knocked.

INT. APARTMENT HALLWAY — CONTINUOUS

The woman from the bar stood outside holding a paper bag.

CAEL
Do you have a name, or does that compromise national enlightenment?

WOMAN
Mara.

CAEL
A real one?

MARA
One I answer to.

She offered him the bag.

MARA
Food.

He didn’t take it.

CAEL
You people have somewhat damaged the restaurant experience for me.

Mara lowered the bag.

For the first time since he’d met her, she appeared to have no prepared response.

CAEL
I want to meet him.

MARA
I know.

CAEL
Tonight.

MARA
He’s downstairs.

Cael glanced past her toward the elevator.

CAEL
Of course he is.

MARA
It’s broken. Take the stairs.

INT. STAIRWELL — MOMENTS LATER

On the landing between the second and third floors, a man knelt beside a disassembled stairlift.

A small flashlight was held between his teeth. His screwdriver kept slipping.

No white suit. No guards. No enormous window overlooking a civilization he was disappointed in.

Just a man in an old black sweater trying to retrieve a screw from beneath a rail.

Cael stopped three steps above him.

CAEL
Tell me you’re the maintenance guy.

The man removed the flashlight.

FOUNDER
Tonight’s going badly enough without adding fraud.

Cael looked back at Mara.

She remained at the top of the stairs.

CAEL
This is him?

FOUNDER
You sound like you paid for better seats.

Cael descended one step.

CAEL
You froze my accounts.

The founder set down the screwdriver.

CAEL
You interfered with my company. You recruited my girlfriend. You put something in my head—or took something out—and then sent me inspirational fucking stationery.

A door opened on the landing above.

MRS. VALE
Some of us are trying to sleep.

Cael looked up.

The founder looked up.

Mara took one quiet step backward.

CAEL
Sorry, Mrs. Vale.

MRS. VALE
And stop upsetting the repairman.

Her door closed.

Cael turned back.

CAEL
You’re enjoying this.

FOUNDER
Parts of it.

CAEL
Stand up.

The founder stood.

He was less imposing upright.

Somehow, this made Cael angrier.

CAEL
I had a speech prepared.

FOUNDER
You can still—

CAEL
Don’t be accommodating. It’s revolting.

Silence settled between them.

At last, the founder nodded.

FOUNDER
All right.

CAEL
Were you going to apologize?

FOUNDER
Yes.

CAEL
Before or after I became grateful?

That landed.

Mara watched the founder carefully.

FOUNDER
Before, I hope.

CAEL
You don’t get to count intentions. Not with me.

INT. STAIRWELL — LATER

Cael sat on the third step. The founder sat on the landing.

Between them lay the opened stairlift motor.

Mara had gone downstairs to get coffee, leaving the paper bag beside Cael.

He hadn’t touched it.

CAEL
She told me I could manifest my desired reality.

FOUNDER
Mara believes—

CAEL
I’m asking you.

The founder rubbed a black streak of grease off his thumb. It spread.

FOUNDER
I think you’re unusually good at making people participate in what you want.

CAEL
That’s called having a company.

FOUNDER
Sometimes.

CAEL
And the rest? The universe arranging itself around me?

FOUNDER
I don’t know.

Cael laughed once.

There was no amusement in it.

CAEL
That is a magnificent thing to say after the drugs.

The founder looked down.

FOUNDER
Yes.

For a moment, Cael felt the peculiar disappointment of hitting a door that was already open.

He had wanted a tyrant.

A tyrant would have made the next part easy.

CAEL
Do you actually think you’re better than me?

The founder considered this long enough to irritate him.

FOUNDER
At some things.

CAEL
There he is.

FOUNDER
And I’ve now made it very difficult to find out which ones.

Cael glanced at the broken machine.

CAEL
We can eliminate electrical engineering.

FOUNDER
Mechanical fault.

CAEL
Then we can eliminate that.

From downstairs came the sound of Mara laughing.

The founder reached for the motor housing.

Cael put a foot on it.

CAEL
If I beat you, what happens?

FOUNDER
At what?

CAEL
Your thing. Judgment. Leadership. Whatever competition you’ve built where the trophy is having to worry about everyone.

FOUNDER
What do you think should happen?

CAEL
You work for me.

Mara reappeared below them.

The founder didn’t look toward her.

FOUNDER
Possibly.

CAEL
Your people follow my instructions.

FOUNDER
They’d have to decide that themselves.

CAEL
Convenient.

FOUNDER
Would you prefer I owned them?

Cael lifted his foot off the housing.

CAEL
I’d prefer a straight answer.

The founder leaned back against the wall.

For the first time, he looked tired enough to be the man Mara had described crying in bed.

FOUNDER
If you can do this better, I want to help you do it.

CAEL
And who decides whether I can?

No answer.

Cael smiled.

There it was.

The hidden room beneath the cathedral.

CAEL
You.

Mara handed the founder a coffee.

He didn’t take it.

CAEL
You select the candidates. Design the ordeal. Interpret the results. Then decide whether someone’s finally worthy of replacing you.

MARA
There are independent—

The founder raised his hand, then stopped himself and lowered it.

Mara noticed.

So did Cael.

FOUNDER
Finish.

MARA
There are independent reviewers.

CAEL
Who selected them?

Mara held both coffees.

The stairwell light went out.

Nobody moved.

CAEL
Very theatrical.

FOUNDER
Motion sensor.

Cael waved one hand.

The light returned.

INT. STAIRWELL — 3:06 A.M.

The terms were written on the back of Mrs. Vale’s takeaway menu.

Cael insisted on writing them himself.

No more chemicals.

No contact with his girlfriend through the organization.

His money returned.

Access to records concerning his recruitment.

The founder read the list.

FOUNDER
Some records contain other people’s identities.

CAEL
Redact the names. Leave what they did.

Mara crouched beside them.

MARA
I can start the release tonight.

Cael looked at her.

CAEL
You could have done that before?

MARA
I could have requested it.

CAEL
That isn’t what I asked.

She absorbed this.

MARA
Yes.

Cael handed her the pen.

CAEL
Then you sign too.

She did.

The founder signed beneath her.

Cael studied the signatures.

They looked disappointingly like ordinary signatures.

FOUNDER
Anything else?

CAEL
Yes. The next test is mine.

Mara’s expression tightened.

The founder waited.

CAEL
You don’t get to know when it starts.

FOUNDER
All right.

CAEL
Or what I’m measuring.

FOUNDER
All right.

CAEL
And if you lose, I want everyone who’s been through your program to hear about it.

The founder held out his hand for the pen.

INT. STAIRWELL — 3:24 A.M.

Cael was almost back at his apartment when the stairlift made a grinding noise.

Then a sharp electrical click.

Then the founder said something that did not belong in cyberscripture.

Cael stopped.

Looked at his door.

Looked down the stairs.

CAEL
Have you checked the track?

FOUNDER
Yes.

A pause.

CAEL
Have you checked it competently?

Five minutes later, Cael lay on the landing with one arm under the rail.

The founder held the flashlight.

Mara stood over them reading a maintenance manual on her phone.

MARA
It says not to operate with the protective housing—

CAEL
We’re not operating it.

MARA
I’m enjoying having instructions.

Cael felt something wedged behind the lower wheel.

He worked it loose.

A small blue plastic knight emerged between his fingers.

Everyone looked at it.

CAEL
Please tell me this isn’t a metaphor.

The founder started laughing.

He tried to stop.

Failed.

Cael sat up, holding the chess piece like evidence.

CAEL
You dismantled the motor.

The founder nodded.

CAEL
For a horse.

The founder covered his face.

Mara sat down on the stairs.

Soon she was laughing too.

Cael tried to maintain his contempt.

It survived almost four seconds.

INT. THIRD-FLOOR LANDING — 3:51 A.M.

Mrs. Vale rode the repaired stairlift up from the second floor wearing a dressing gown and the severe expression of a queen dissatisfied with her procession.

The three of them watched.

At the top, she pressed the stop button.

MRS. VALE
You woke me up to demonstrate that you’ve finished waking me up.

FOUNDER
We needed to test it under—

MRS. VALE
Choose your next word carefully.

CAEL
Realistic operating conditions.

She looked at Cael.

MRS. VALE
You should be in sales.

CAEL
Recovering.

She rose, took the blue knight from his hand, and inspected it.

MRS. VALE
My grandson’s.

Cael glanced at the founder.

CAEL
Is he with you as well?

MRS. VALE
He’s six.

CAEL
That wasn’t a no.

She went into her apartment.

A moment later, the door opened again.

MRS. VALE
There’s soup. If you’re all finished being strange.

INT. MRS. VALE’S KITCHEN — BEFORE DAWN

Her kitchen was too small for four people.

The founder sat on a folding chair. Mara’s knees pressed against a cupboard. Cael ate soup from a bowl with a chipped blue rim.

Nobody discussed the future of civilization.

Mrs. Vale talked about a tree the council intended to remove.

The founder began suggesting an appeal.

Mara asked whether anyone had inspected the roots.

Cael asked who owned the land.

Mrs. Vale placed her spoon down.

MRS. VALE
I was going to say it’s a shame. My husband planted it.

They fell quiet.

MRS. VALE
You can let me finish before saving it.

Outside, the first delivery truck rattled along the street.

Cael looked at the founder across the little table.

Without the dossier, without the voice on the telephone, without Mara explaining what his suffering meant, he was simply a man being corrected by an elderly woman.

He listened.

Cael found that harder to dismiss than anything he had said.

INT. CAEL’S APARTMENT — DAWN

At his door, Mara handed him a receipt.

The release request. A reference number. Her signature.

Something he could check.

MARA
You’ll have the first records tomorrow.

He nodded.

The founder waited beside the stairs.

FOUNDER
When should I expect your test?

Cael considered him.

CAEL
Go home.

FOUNDER
That’s it?

CAEL
For tonight.

The founder began to speak, then apparently thought better of it.

He left.

Mara followed.

Cael locked his door.

He put the agreement on his desk, beside the returned four hundred dollars.

Then he opened a notebook.

At the top of a clean page, he wrote:

TEST 1

Below it:

Can he leave something unfinished in someone else’s hands?

Cael listened to the footsteps descending.

They paused on the landing.

For a moment, he expected them to come back.

Instead, the front door opened.

And closed.

Cael added one word.

Apparently.

He went to bed before he could turn it into praise.

For the first time in weeks, there was nothing waiting under his pillow.

He checked anyway.

CUT TO BLACK.

END OF EPISODE 1

Season 0, Episode 2: “What Survives”

INT. INTERBEAUTIFUL — RECORDS ROOM — NIGHT

The founder had been reading about himself for forty-seven minutes.

He wasn’t enjoying it.

Across the table, Cael watched him turn another page.

CAEL
Anything good?

FOUNDER
Apparently I dislike surprises.

CAEL
Whoever wrote that must be having a wonderful evening.

The room was below a closed public library. Exposed pipes. Filing cabinets. An expensive scanner sitting on a desk repaired with a cheap metal bracket.

The founder tapped a paragraph.

FOUNDER
I don’t remember approving this.

CAEL
That seems to be a recurring management issue.

FOUNDER
I remember approving your account freeze. Six hours. Last Thursday. They told me the outbound transfer was going to a compromised recipient.

Cael’s expression changed.

CAEL
It was.

The founder looked up.

CAEL
Compromised doesn’t mean optional.

He placed his phone on the table.

A photograph showed a long brick building beside the water.

The next showed the same building without its roof.

The third showed a yellow excavator standing where the entrance had been.

CAEL
The Calder. Recording rooms downstairs. Rehearsal space above. Forty-three tenants. My father wired the original mixing desk.

The founder set down the report.

CAEL
The owner died. His son wanted it sold. I had a purchase agreement, a deadline, and enough money to meet it.

FOUNDER
And the transfer failed.

CAEL
At four fifty-eight.

Silence.

CAEL
Your people restored access at ten. The seller had already accepted a backup offer. They started demolition yesterday.

The founder looked at the photographs again.

FOUNDER
Was anyone inside?

CAEL
No.

A small release of breath.

CAEL
You don’t get to be that relieved. Nobody dying isn’t the same as nothing happening.

FOUNDER
I know.

CAEL
There were marks on a doorframe. My height. Every summer until I was sixteen.

The founder slid the report away.

FOUNDER
I’m sorry.

CAEL
I can afford another building.

FOUNDER
Yes.

CAEL
So don’t offer me one.

FOUNDER
I won’t.

Somewhere overhead, water moved through an old pipe.

CAEL
I kept thinking I’d buy it when I had enough. Then I had enough, and I kept making more.

The founder waited.

CAEL
I was finally doing it.

Mara entered carrying three visitor badges and a sealed document pouch.

She saw the photographs.

Stopped.

MARA
Cael—

CAEL
Did you know?

MARA
I knew about the purchase. I was told the deadline had been extended.

CAEL
By whom?

She opened the pouch.

A single sheet came out.

MARA
That’s what I came down to ask.

It was the freeze authorization.

Beneath the founder’s signature:

RECIPIENT EXPOSURE CONFIRMED. NO IRREVERSIBLE CONSEQUENCES ANTICIPATED.

Cael read it twice.

CAEL
Well. Good anticipation.

The founder stood.

FOUNDER
We need the original assessment.

MARA
I’ve requested it.

FOUNDER
Who wrote it?

MARA
The field is blank.

CAEL
Of course it is.

The founder’s gaze remained on the page.

FOUNDER
No. That field can’t be blank.

INT. RECORDS ROOM — LATER

Mara went upstairs to retrieve the access logs.

Cael stayed seated.

CAEL
How much do you actually remember?

The founder turned toward him immediately.

Something in his face opened.

FOUNDER
Good question.

Cael blinked.

CAEL
You look pleased.

FOUNDER
You’re asking how it works.

The founder pulled his chair closer.

FOUNDER
I remember making the original recordings. Ali had the phone. I remember wanting the proposal evaluated. Later, there was an operational period. That’s where the gaps are.

CAEL
You remember volunteering, but not everything you volunteered for.

FOUNDER
Exactly.

He said it with such sudden enthusiasm that Cael almost smiled.

FOUNDER
They brought me back in nineteen days ago. Since then I’ve been reading records, meeting people, checking what survived without me.

CAEL
And signing things.

FOUNDER
Too quickly.

Cael glanced at the authorization.

CAEL
Why would you trust an organization you couldn’t remember?

The founder leaned forward.

FOUNDER
Yes. That’s the question.

CAEL
I’m accusing you.

FOUNDER
You’re also right.

He took a pen and wrote on the report’s cover.

CAEL
What are you writing?

FOUNDER
Your question.

CAEL
You could answer it.

The pen stopped.

FOUNDER
I wanted it to have worked.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Cael reached across the table and turned the report toward himself.

INT. LIBRARY — MAIN FLOOR — NIGHT

Mara found Cael beside an empty display case.

Inside it, a faded card read:

PLEASE ASK FOR ASSISTANCE.

CAEL
Does he do that with everyone?

MARA
What?

CAEL
Look delighted when they find a hole in him.

MARA
When it’s a real question.

CAEL
What’s a fake one?

MARA
You asked me at the bar whether I believed in love at first sight.

CAEL
A subject of continuing research.

MARA
You were looking at your reflection behind my head.

CAEL
Multidisciplinary research.

She almost laughed.

He noticed.

Of course he noticed.

He stepped closer, lowered his voice.

CAEL
There you are.

Mara held his gaze.

MARA
There I am what?

CAEL
Off duty.

MARA
No.

The word was gentle enough to stop him more effectively than a rebuke.

MARA
You’re attractive. You know what your voice does. You know exactly how long to look at someone before looking away.

Cael waited.

MARA
I work in an industry full of beautiful people who make attention feel like a gift. You’re very good. You’re not a new species.

CAEL
That sounded rehearsed.

MARA
I’ve had time.

He stepped back.

CAEL
And him?

Her eyes moved toward the basement door.

MARA
I loved him before there was a place like this to stand in.

CAEL
Past tense?

MARA
No.

That answer cost her something. She let him see it.

MARA
There were evenings he told me things he no longer remembers telling anyone. I remember them alone now.

Cael’s expression softened.

MARA
But I can’t make what we had into a debt he owes me.

The basement door opened.

The founder appeared carrying two folders.

FOUNDER
Mara. I need you with Cael tomorrow.

She went still.

MARA
I was supposed to go through the recordings with you.

FOUNDER
I know.

MARA
Someone else can take him.

FOUNDER
Someone else can play me a recording. You know the people who handled his case.

She looked down at the badges in her hand.

Then nodded.

MARA
All right.

The founder hesitated.

FOUNDER
Thank you.

He returned downstairs.

Cael watched her compose herself.

For once, he didn’t try to help by being charming.

MARA
I’m going with you because this matters. If you want to know whether there could ever be anything between us, stop performing interest in it. Find out whether you actually care.

CAEL
And if I do?

MARA
Then you’ll have something worth doing regardless of my answer.

INT. RECORDS ROOM — 11:42 P.M.

The founder had covered the table with documents.

Cael stood beside him.

CAEL
Who bought the Calder?

FOUNDER
A company called North Quay Holdings.

CAEL
Who owns that?

The founder looked up, visibly energized.

FOUNDER
Yes. Pull that thread.

CAEL
You’re like this when someone asks where the bathroom is, aren’t you?

FOUNDER
Depends how long they’ve been afraid to ask.

Cael took the laptop.

For several minutes, the only sounds were typing and the founder opening drawers.

Then Cael stopped.

CAEL
Oh.

He rotated the screen.

An investment company. A list of directors.

At the top:

ADRIAN VOSS.

The founder didn’t recognize the name.

Cael did.

CAEL
He offered to buy my company twice.

FOUNDER
What does he do?

CAEL
Buys things people can’t afford to lose.

Mara came down the stairs.

MARA
Voss underwrites three of our regional facilities.

The founder looked at her.

MARA
Through intermediaries. It’s in the funding review.

FOUNDER
Which review?

MARA
The one you haven’t reached yet.

Cael laughed quietly.

CAEL
He’s paying for the filing cabinets you’re using to investigate him.

The access log contained a timestamp.

The risk assessment had been amended eleven minutes before the founder signed it.

The earlier version contained a warning:

MISSED TRANSFER MAY TRIGGER PERMANENT LOSS OF PURCHASE RIGHTS.

Someone had removed it.

In its place, the assurance that no irreversible consequences were anticipated.

The founder read both versions.

Then read them again.

His excitement was gone.

CAEL
Still pleased I asked?

FOUNDER
Yes.

He looked at Cael.

FOUNDER
Very.

Mara’s phone vibrated.

A message appeared from the upstairs desk.

DETECTIVE HERE FOR CAEL. SAYS HE CALLED SIX TIMES.

Cael closed his eyes.

CAEL
Right. I reported you people.

FOUNDER
Sensible.

INT. LIBRARY — ENTRANCE — MIDNIGHT

Detective Inez Rook wore a raincoat and the expression of someone who had already worked tomorrow’s hours.

ROOK
You told dispatch a private organization was interfering with your finances.

Cael looked back.

The founder stood several paces away, holding the original authorization.

Mara was beside him.

CAEL
Never mind. It’s—

Rook took out a notebook.

ROOK
If the next word is “complicated,” I’m going to need you to appreciate how little that narrows my profession.

Cael glanced at the founder.

The founder said nothing.

Didn’t rescue him. Didn’t signal.

Cael turned back.

CAEL
Never mind my explanation of who was behind it.

He held out the two assessments.

CAEL
I still want you to look at what happened.

Rook read.

Then she looked at the founder.

ROOK
Your signature?

FOUNDER
Yes.

ROOK
And you’re giving me this voluntarily?

FOUNDER
Yes.

ROOK
That’ll save us an annoying conversation.

She handed Cael a card.

ROOK
Keep the originals. Send me copies. And stop withdrawing complaints before I arrive. It makes the drive feel personal.

INT. RECORDS ROOM — AFTER MIDNIGHT

Cael’s phone rang.

He looked at the name.

Then put it on speaker.

CAEL
Adrian.

VOSS (PHONE)
I heard about the Calder.

CAEL
You bought it.

VOSS
One of my companies did.

CAEL
Did one of your companies know my transfer wouldn’t clear?

A small silence.

VOSS
You’re upset. Understandably. Come see me tomorrow. We should discuss what you’re getting involved in.

The founder pulled a blank sheet toward him.

CAEL
Tell me now.

VOSS
They’ve built something remarkable. People who cooperate without needing credit. People willing to abandon profitable positions because someone has shown them a better use for their abilities.

Voss sounded almost admiring.

VOSS
Do you understand what that workforce is worth?

Mara’s jaw tightened.

The founder wrote one word.

Cael read it.

ASK.

CAEL
Worth to whom?

The founder looked up at him.

Despite everything, that sudden brightness returned.

VOSS
To whoever can give it direction.

CAEL
They have a mission.

VOSS
They have several buildings with leases.

Nobody moved.

VOSS
Tomorrow. Ten o’clock. You still know how to recognize an opportunity, I hope.

The call ended.

For a moment, the room seemed smaller.

Then Mara opened her laptop.

MARA
I’m checking every lease.

The founder nodded.

Cael remained beside the phone.

CAEL
I spent ten years wanting him to think I was worth buying.

INT. LIBRARY — BEFORE DAWN

They moved upstairs when the basement became too cold.

Mara called the regional offices.

The founder sorted copies for Rook.

Cael began contacting the Calder’s former tenants.

At the seventh call, someone answered.

He stepped away.

CAEL
Yeah. It’s me. Listen, I can’t get the building back.

A pause.

CAEL
No. I’m not calling to explain why.

He looked at the library’s unused meeting rooms.

CAEL
How many of you still need somewhere to work?

Across the room, the founder watched him.

Cael covered the phone.

CAEL
Don’t look so fucking pleased. I haven’t asked permission yet.

FOUNDER
Neither have I.

CAEL
Who owns this place?

The founder grinned.

Cael pointed at him.

CAEL
It’s a practical question. Control yourself.

By morning, they had three appointments, a list of threatened facilities, and seventeen people willing to help recover what remained of the Calder’s community.

The building was gone.

Cael didn’t forgive the founder.

He did pull a chair up beside him.

CAEL
I don’t want a position where I have to wait for you to notice something’s wrong.

The founder turned a fresh page.

FOUNDER
What do you need?

Cael thought.

A real thought, allowed to take its time.

Then he began.

INT. VOSS’S OFFICE — MORNING

High above the river, Adrian Voss watched demolition footage on a silent screen.

His assistant stood in the doorway.

ASSISTANT
Cael declined the meeting.

Voss paused the video.

VOSS
Has he joined them?

ASSISTANT
We don’t know.

VOSS
Find out whether he’s asked for a title.

ASSISTANT
Why?

Voss studied the frozen image of the excavator.

VOSS
If he wants a title, we can still afford him.

CUT TO BLACK.

END OF EPISODE 2
